### What does Conflict Tracker do?

Conflict Tracker reads news from thousands of outlets in dozens of languages every morning (UTC, plus the GDELT event database and official records such as US arms-sale notices, export-enforcement actions, and UN panel of experts reports.

AI models translate each report, keep the ones that describe a military, political, or arms trade event, write a short summary, name the actors, and place the event on the map. Reports of the same event are merged only when a checking model confirms they match, and the event keeps every source: a visit to Kyiv on 4 Oct 2026 is one event with 62 sources. In the week of 28 Sep–4 Oct 2026 the map held 12,090 events.&#x20;

The site also publishes daily briefs for 11 conflict theaters and 106 countries, actor pages, and an arms trade tracker that records each deal's supplier, recipient, stage, systems, and value.

**The lowdown:** Its main value is taking a large volume of reporting and turning it into geographically organised events that can be followed back to the original sources.

### Use in Reporting

Conflict Tracker can be used to:&#x20;

* Find other outlets, including ones in other languages, that reported an incident, and read their reports translated into English.
* Check where and when an incident was first reported, and by whom.
* See what was reported in an area over the past week, or inside an area drawn on the map.
* Follow an arms deal from approval to contract to delivery, with US arms-sale notices and export-license reports alongside the news.
* Start a daily scan of a conflict from its theater brief, which cites its sources. Example: [https://conflict-tracker.com/conflicts/sudan](https://conflict-tracker.com/conflicts/sudan)&#x20;

| **Capabilities**                                                                                          | **Limitations**                                                                                                                                                                           |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Events from thousands of news outlets in dozens of languages, translated into English.                    | Summaries and briefs are written by AI models. Automated checks test them against the original reporting, but errors remain possible; check important details against the linked sources. |
| One event per incident, keeping every outlet that reported it.                                            | The checks test whether the site represents its sources accurately, not whether a reported claim is true.                                                                                 |
| Each event's date, place, type (military, political, arms trade), actors, and source links.               | Updated once a day, not in real time.                                                                                                                                                     |
| Places looked up only inside the country the report names; doubtful points reviewed again after each run. | Coverage is thinner where few outlets report.                                                                                                                                             |
| Daily briefs for 11 conflict theaters and 106 countries, citing their sources.                            | <p>Some events can be placed only at city or country level.</p><p><br></p>                                                                                                                |
| Arms trade tracker: supplier, recipient, stage, systems, and value, plus official records.                | The record has a gap from late May to late September 2026, partly backfilled.                                                                                                             |
| Actor pages for military units, armed groups, government bodies, and companies.                           | Source links, older dates, and filters need a free account.                                                                                                                               |

### Summary

Conflict Tracker is most useful early in an investigation: discovering what has been reported about an incident or area, and by which outlets, across languages. Its strengths are breadth, one event per incident with all its sources, and careful placement. Its weaknesses are that summaries are AI-written, it updates daily rather than live, and the full source list needs a free account.&#x20;

**Note:** Treat it as a pointer to the original reporting, not a verified record.

### Ethical Considerations

* Events report claims made by parties to a conflict; read them as claims, not established facts.
* Linked reports can describe or show violence, casualties, and identifiable people.
* Do not republish AI summaries as verified facts; confirm details with the original reporting first.
* Names of people come from published news reports; take care before amplifying personal details.
