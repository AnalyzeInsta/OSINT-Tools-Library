### What does Time.Graphics do?

Time.Graphics lets users create interactive timelines showing events in chronological order. Each event can include a date, description and supporting material, such as images, videos, hyperlinks, files or geographic locations.

The platform also supports data imports, including Google Calendar and Google Sheets, and provides options for exporting timelines into formats suitable for presentations, reports or offline use.

For OSINT investigators, the main benefit is the ability to turn information collected from multiple sources into a structured chronology. This can make sequences of events, overlaps, gaps and changes easier to identify and explain.

**The lowdown:** It’s a timeline-building and evidence-organisation tool.&#x20;

### Use in Reporting

Time.Graphics can be useful for:

* Building a chronology of events in a criminal, corporate, political, environmental or conflict investigation.
* Organising incidents, public statements, court proceedings and official announcements by date.
* Comparing the timelines of multiple individuals, organisations or related events.
* Identifying gaps, overlaps and inconsistencies in reported sequences of events.
* Attaching source links and supporting documents to individual timeline entries.
* Organising information collected from public records, news articles, social media and other OSINT sources.
* Creating visual chronologies for investigative reports, presentations and editorial meetings.
* Collaborating with colleagues on a shared chronology, where appropriate access controls are available.
* Importing existing event data from supported sources, such as Google Calendar or Google Sheets.
* Presenting complex investigations in a format that readers can follow and independently check.

### Summary

Time.Graphics is a timeline visualisation and evidence-organisation platform that helps turn scattered information into a chronological narrative. It’s particularly useful for organising complex investigations, documenting the sequence of events and presenting findings clearly.

Its strongest role is in the analysis and reporting stages of an OSINT workflow, although it can support collection by allowing source links and supporting material to be attached to individual events.&#x20;

**Note:** It should complement, not replace, source verification and evidence preservation.

### Ethical Considerations

* A timeline can make unverified claims appear authoritative. Check significant entries against original sources.
* Do not expose confidential sources, personal data or sensitive investigative details in public timelines.
* Confirm whether a timeline is public, private or accessible by link before adding sensitive information.
* Missing events do not necessarily indicate that nothing happened, and temporal proximity does not prove causation.
