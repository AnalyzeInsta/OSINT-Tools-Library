### What does Airnav do?

AirNav provides a searchable database of US airports and airfields, ranging from major public airports to private airstrips, heliports, seaplane bases, balloonports and other aviation facilities.&#x20;

Information includes coordinates, elevation, operational information, communications, runway information and ownership/management details.

**The lowdown:** Its main value is being able to start with an airport name or identifier and build a structured picture of the facility.

### How to Use:

**1. Search for an airport using its name, FAA identifier, city, ZIP code or geographic coordinates. Alternatively, use AirNav's airport search filters to identify airfields matching particular characteristics.**

### Use in Reporting

AirNav can be useful for:

* Identifying airports and airfields from FAA identifiers or names.
* Establishing the geographic location and coordinates of an aviation facility.
* Identifying whether an airport is publicly or privately owned.
* Identifying airport owners and managers listed in the available FAA information.
* Investigating runway dimensions, surfaces, headings and physical characteristics.
* Identifying airport communications frequencies and relevant air-traffic facilities.
* Identifying nearby VORs, NDBs and other radio-navigation infrastructure.
* Researching airport services, fuel availability and operational facilities.
* Identifying instrument approach procedures associated with an airport.
* Establishing aviation infrastructure around a particular location.
* Supporting investigations involving private airstrips, aviation facilities or aircraft movements.

The platform has been cited in numerous NTSB aviation investigations including a 2009 [Cessna P206 accident in Oregon](https://data.ntsb.gov/Docket/TOCPrintable?data=69371&) which included a four-page AirNav-sourced airport-information document in the official docket.&#x20;

| **Capabilities**                                                                   | **Limitations**                                                                                             |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Searchable database of US airports and airfields.                                  | Focused on US aviation information.                                                                         |
| Provides coordinates, elevation, airport identifiers and operational information.  | Information can change and should be checked against the current FAA source for important claims.           |
| Provides runway, communications and navigation-aid information.                    | Should not automatically be treated as the authoritative source where an official FAA record is available.  |
| Provides ownership and management information from FAA records.                    | Airport ownership information does not establish who actually operates or uses an aircraft.                 |
| Provides instrument procedures, services and related aviation information.         | Operational information such as weather, NOTAMs and procedures is time-sensitive.                           |

### Summary

AirNav is a free US aviation OSINT and infrastructure-research tool providing detailed information about airports and airfields, particularly useful for establishing the identity, location, ownership, physical characteristics and operational environment of an aviation facility.&#x20;

The platform is strongest during discovery and collection in an OSINT investigation but significant ownership, operational or investigative claims should be verified against the underlying FAA records and other primary aviation sources.&#x20;

### Ownership

AirNav is operated by AirNav, LLC, which its [privacy policy](https://www.airnav.com/info/privacy.html) identifies as a New Jersey Limited Liability Company.

### Ethical Considerations

* Verify important information against current FAA or airport records.
* Remember that airport ownership does not establish aircraft ownership or aircraft-user identity.
* Treat private-airfield information carefully where publication could expose sensitive location or security information.
* Do not assume that an aircraft being associated with an airport proves that it was present there at a particular time.
* Distinguish between airport infrastructure information and actual aircraft movement data.
