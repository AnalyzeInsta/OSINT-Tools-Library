### What does ScreenshotChecker do?

ScreenshotChecker provides a collection of browser-based tools for examining screenshots and other images as potential digital evidence. It includes a screenshot text-extraction workspace and an image redactor.&#x20;

The OCR output can be reviewed and edited. The redactor offers manual Blackout and Whiteout rectangles, sensitive-information suggestions, and an Export Clean PNG action. This submission concerns that preparation workflow, rather than the site's broader image-analysis or link-checking tools.

The investigative use is to preserve the original screenshot privately while preparing an appropriately redacted copy for a report or other authorised sharing.&#x20;

**Note:** OCR and masking suggestions need human review. These functions do not establish who created a screenshot, whether a message was sent, whether money arrived, or whether the depicted event occurred.

**The lowdown:** Its main analysis can extract text using browser-based OCR, inspect available EXIF and other metadata, identify potentially sensitive information, examine suspicious URLs and look for image-manipulation indicators such as compression differences and ELA patterns.&#x20;

### How to Use:

**1. Preserve the original screenshot and relevant source context. Work on a sharing copy. Open the site and choose Select Screenshot.**

### Use in Reporting

ScreenshotChecker can be useful for:

* Extracting readable text from screenshots using browser-based OCR.
* Identifying potentially exposed personal information such as email addresses, phone numbers, payment-card numbers, addresses and other sensitive data.
* Inspecting available image metadata, including timestamps, software information and GPS data where present.
* Investigating screenshots for potential editing or manipulation indicators using ELA and compression analysis.
* Extracting and examining URLs, hostnames and other web destinations visible in screenshots.
* Comparing two screenshots or images to identify visual differences or possible alterations.
* Preparing screenshots for publication by redacting sensitive information before sharing.
* Preparing image copies for reverse-image searching and tracing the wider context or source of an image.
* Preserving the original evidence while creating a separate redacted or investigative working copy.
* Generating investigative leads that can then be checked against original webpages, applications, transaction records or other authoritative sources.

**Important Note:** ScreenshotChecker itself stresses that these checks do not prove that a screenshot is genuine or that an event represented in the image actually happened. Important claims should therefore be independently verified.&#x20;

### Summary

ScreenshotChecker is useful for the preparation stage of a screenshot-based report: transcribe text, inspect suggestions and create a separately redacted copy. The practical strength is keeping these steps in one browser workflow.&#x20;

Its main limitations are OCR error, incomplete suggestions and the need to verify the saved image, metadata and the site's privacy behavior for the intended sensitivity. It should not be presented as a definitive authenticity or fraud detector.

### Ethical Considerations

* Keep an unchanged original and describe edits when a redacted image is published.
* Minimise disclosure and consider whether remaining context could identify someone.
* Use images you have the right to process and share and do not use OCR or metadata to justify unsupported allegations.
* Check important claims against the original source, account or service.
* Consider your organisation's sensitive-data policy and review browser storage after cross-tool use.
