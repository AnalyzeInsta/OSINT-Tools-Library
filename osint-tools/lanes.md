# Lanes

| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| URL              | [https://lanes.news](https://lanes.news)                                                                                                                                         |
| What it does     | Compares how each party's press, and press from uninvolved countries, report the same conflict events, and labels each claim as corroborated, framed, disputed or single-source. |
| How to use it    | Pick a conflict, open today's daily page or the bridge page, and follow each claim to its source articles.                                                                       |

### What does Lanes do?

For eight conflict pairs, Lanes pulls coverage from each party's press, including domestic outlets read in the original language, and from press in countries outside the conflict. It groups articles about the same events and sorts each claim: corroborated (marked independent or same origin), framed differently, disputed, or reported by one side only.&#x20;

A rule check demotes any "corroborated" claim without citations from both sides. Daily articles are compiled by AI (Claude) from the cited reporting and labelled as not human-reviewed.

**The lowdown:** It’s useful at the start of conflict research, to see where the accounts of each side overlap and where they don't.

### Cookies:&#x20;

The site uses Google Analytics 4 cookies for website usage measurement. Testing identified \_ga, which distinguishes visitors for Analytics purposes, and \_ga\_8XLKHHD7C4, which persists session state for the relevant GA4 property. These are first-party cookies because they are set on .lanes.news, although the analytics service is provided by Google.&#x20;

### Use in Reporting

Lanes can be useful for:

* Comparing how opposing media environments report the same event.
* Identifying facts or claims reported by multiple sides and third-party sources.
* Finding disputed or single-source claims that require further verification.
* Identifying differences in terminology, naming and framing between media outlets.
* Quickly locating original articles and tracing a claim back to its source.
* Supporting conflict monitoring, media analysis and investigative research.
* Using Lanes' open datasets for research, reporting or analysis, with the required attribution.

| **Capabilities**                                          | **Limitations**                                             |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Reads domestic-language press on both sides.              | Only covers eight conflict pairs.                           |
| Labels claims as corroborated, disputed or single-source. | Shows what was published, not what happened on the ground.  |
| Flags "corroboration" that traces back to one origin.     | AI-compiled daily articles are not human-reviewed.          |
| Links every claim to its source article.                  | Depends on outlets with working feeds; some lanes are thin. |

### Summary

Lanes is useful at the start of conflict research, to see where the accounts of each side overlap and where they don't, and it fits primarily into the discovery, verification and analysis stages of the OSINT workflow.&#x20;

Its main value is reducing the time needed to compare competing accounts while keeping the underlying sources visible. However, it should not replace examination of the original reporting or independent verification, particularly for consequential claims.&#x20;

**Important caveat:** It shows what was published, not what happened on the ground, and AI-compiled daily articles are not human-reviewed.

### Ownership

### Ethical Considerations

* Check claims against the linked source articles before relying on them.
* State-funded outlets are included on all sides and labelled.
* Overlap between sides is evidence, not proof.
* Be cautious when interpreting differences in terminology as evidence of deliberate propaganda or deception.
