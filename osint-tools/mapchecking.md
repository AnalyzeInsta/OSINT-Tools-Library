### What does MapChecking do?

MapChecking helps users estimate the number of people who could fit within a specified area. It combines the geographic area drawn on a map with an assumed crowd density to calculate an estimated headcount.

It’s useful when crowd-size claims made by event organisers, authorities, politicians or media outlets differ. By making the calculation and assumptions visible, it gives journalists and researchers a way to test whether a reported figure is plausible.

**Important Note:** The tool does not automatically count individual people in a photograph or video.&#x20;

**The lowdown:** It provides a transparent, repeatable way to estimate the number of people who could occupy a particular space.&#x20;

### How to Use:

**1. Find the location by searching for the relevant square, street, park or other public space. Ideally, first geolocate the photograph or video being investigated so the mapped location corresponds to the scene.**

<img src="../.gitbook/assets/unknown (728).png" alt="" height="389" width="602">

**2. Use the map to draw a polygon around the space actually occupied by the crowd. Switch to satellite imagery where useful, and exclude buildings, water, inaccessible areas, barriers and other spaces that were not occupied. Compare the polygon with the original photograph or footage to check that the boundaries match.**

<img src="../.gitbook/assets/unknown (729).png" alt="" height="535" width="602">

<img src="../.gitbook/assets/unknown (730).png" alt="" height="547" width="602">

**3. Estimate crowd density and calculate the total. Use close-up photographs or clear footage to estimate the number of people per square metre. Adjust the density control and examine how the estimated total changes. Where the density is uncertain, calculate a plausible low and high estimate instead of presenting one number as exact.**

### Use in Reporting

MapChecking can be useful for:

* Fact-checking claims about attendance at protests, demonstrations, rallies and public gatherings.
* Estimating the number of people who could occupy a defined square, street, park or other public space.
* Testing whether an organiser's, politician's, authority's or media outlet's attendance figure is physically plausible.
* Comparing alternative crowd-size estimates using different assumptions about density.
* Developing a plausible range of attendance figures where the available imagery does not support a precise count.
* Supporting the geolocation and verification of crowd photographs and videos.
* Explaining the methodology and assumptions behind a reported crowd-size estimate.
* Identifying areas that need further examination because buildings, barriers or uneven crowd distribution affect the calculation.

As a real-world example, [RTL France](https://www.rtl.fr/actu/politique/presidentielle-2022-100-000-militants-au-meeting-de-zemmour-au-trocadero-pourquoi-ce-chiffre-est-conteste-7900138393) published a report questioning the claim that 100,000 people attended the Éric Zemmour rally at the Trocadéro in March 2022. Its article referenced MapChecking and included an estimate based on the area occupied by the crowd and an assumed density.

| **Capabilities**                                                            | **Limitations**                                                                 |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Calculates approximate crowd size from a mapped area and selected density.  | Does not automatically count people visible in a photograph or video.           |
| Allows users to outline irregularly shaped areas with a polygon.            | Results depend on how accurately the occupied area is delineated.               |
| Provides adjustable crowd-density assumptions.                              | Density is subjective and may vary substantially across the same crowd.         |
| Supports checking claims about protest and event attendance.                | A physically possible maximum is not necessarily the actual number present.     |
| Makes it possible to compare estimates under different assumptions.         | Does not independently verify the imagery, date, location or attendance claim.  |

### Summary

MapChecking helps researchers calculate the approximate number of people who could fit within a mapped area, making it useful for examining disputed attendance figures at protests, rallies and other gatherings.

Its strongest role is in verification and analysis stages of the OSINT workflow, after the event location and relevant imagery have been established.&#x20;

**Note:** Results should be reported as estimates or ranges, with the area, density assumptions, source imagery and uncertainty documented.

### Ownership

MapChecking was created by [Anthony Catel](https://x.com/antcatel), a French developer. It’s an independent project rather than a government crowd-counting service.

### Ethical Considerations

* Do not present an estimate as an exact headcount.
* Explain the assumptions behind the mapped area and selected crowd density.
* Account for uneven density, empty space, obstructions and areas outside the camera's field of view.
* Use photographs and video that correspond to the correct location and time.
* Where possible, report a range and compare it with independent evidence.
