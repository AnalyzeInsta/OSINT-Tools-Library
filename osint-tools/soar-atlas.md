### What does Soar Atlas do?

Soar Atlas is a community-driven digital atlas and geospatial imagery platform. It brings together maps, satellite imagery, drone imagery, historical maps, and other geographic content in an interactive interface.

Satellite-image filters can highlight features such as vegetation, water, urban areas, geological formations and heat-related signatures.&#x20;

The site states that its platform contains more than one million maps and satellite images and provides access to imagery from sources including Sentinel and Landsat, alongside community-contributed maps and imagery.&#x20;

**The lowdown:** Its particular value for OSINT is the ability to examine a location from a geographic and temporal perspective as you can compare imagery from different dates to look for changes in buildings, roads, vegetation, infrastructure, terrain or other visible features.&#x20;

### Cookies:&#x20;

The site uses first- and third-party cookies for consent management, analytics, payment functionality and security. During testing, the first-party CookieConsent cookie was observed on soaratlas.com.&#x20;

Third-party cookies included Stripe cookies such as machine\_identifier, private\_machine\_identifier, merchant and site-auth, which support Stripe's payment/security infrastructure, and Microsoft's MUID cookie on clarity.ms, associated with Microsoft Clarity analytics.

### Use in Reporting

Soar Atlas can be useful for:

* Establishing the geographic location and physical characteristics of an area using satellite imagery, maps and other geospatial material.
* Comparing imagery from different dates to identify changes to buildings, infrastructure, roads, vegetation, terrain or other visible features.
* Supporting investigations into conflicts, disasters and major events by providing geographically referenced imagery that can be compared with other evidence.
* Investigating environmental and land-use changes, including flooding, fires, urban development, deforestation and other landscape changes.
* Verifying or challenging imagery circulating online, particularly where the provenance or authenticity of a satellite image is disputed.
* Supporting visual investigations and journalistic reporting through maps, satellite imagery, timelapses, annotations and geographic context.
* Providing geospatial evidence for further analysis, including imagery that can be incorporated into GIS workflows such as QGIS.

Documented real-world examples include [Misbar](https://www.misbar.com/en/editorial/2026/04/10/satellite-images-show-changes-%E2%80%9Ckonish-canyon%E2%80%9D-missile-complex) using Soar Atlas imagery to examine changes at a missile complex in Iran, and the [Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/defense-news/article-899000) reporting using Soar Atlas-published imagery when examining apparent damage at Ramat David Airbase.

### Summary

Soar Atlas is best positioned in the geospatial discovery, verification and analysis stages of the OSINT workflow and is particularly useful after you have identified a location, event or geographic claim and need to establish what the area looked like at a particular time or how it changed.

Its main strength is bringing together maps, satellite imagery and historical geographic information in an accessible interface, however, important findings should be corroborated using the original imagery provider and additional independent sources.

### Ownership

Soar Atlas is operated by Soar.Earth Ltd, with its registered/contact information identifying the company in Osborne Park, Western Australia. [Amir Farhand](https://www.crunchbase.com/person/amir-farhand) is listed as CEO and founder.

### Ethical Considerations

* Distinguish observations from analytical interpretations in reporting.
* Avoid unnecessary identification of private residences or individuals.
* Preserve the original imagery and relevant metadata where possible so that findings can be independently reviewed.
* Be particularly cautious with AI-enhanced or manipulated imagery; Soar itself warns that enhancement can introduce misleading visual details.
* When using community-contributed material, check the contributor, attribution and licence rather than assuming that all imagery has the same provenance.
